Cloth drape (2026-10-02, branch `3d-fit-upgrade`): tops are now draped in a Web Worker after the procedural shape is shown. The garment is rebuilt at about 1.2 cm spacing (about 10,000 particles for the oversized tee) and simulated for 0.9 s of simulated time with XPBD (600 small steps per second, three stretch passes per step, weak bending, friction 0.6). The collider is a signed distance field on an 8 mm grid, built from the mannequin mesh morphed to the current body plus the worn trousers. The trousers' seat, leg and waistband tubes are inflated by 6 mm and the belt loops and fly by 3 mm. Each mesh gets its own field and the fields are combined by taking the minimum. An earlier version measured distances to all triangles in one grid; between the body and the trousers it pushed the shirt toward the body, leaving 55 shirt vertices inside the trousers on the 185 cm broad body. Separate fields fixed that (0 inside). On the mannequin surface the field is 0.46 mm from zero on average. Draping the striped tee over the trousers gave a mean edge stretch of 2.3% (default body) and 2.0% (185 cm broad). No body vertex was uncovered along its surface normal on the torso (0/795 on both bodies). On the shoulder tops 0/236 were uncovered on the default body and 4/246 on the 185 cm broad body (front of the armhole). No shirt vertex ended inside the trousers. One drape took about 1.8–3 s in Node and 1.5–2.1 s in the browser on this Mac.

To make the drape work, the shoulders of the procedural top were rebuilt. Before, the neckline sat at 1.506 m and the shoulder line was a flat shelf about 3.5 cm above the mannequin's shoulders. In the first simulations the shirt slid down about 5 cm and the neckline widened into a boat neck. Rings were spaced evenly in height, so across the nearly flat top of the shoulder one 1.2 cm height step was an 8 cm step outward, and the shoulder poked through between particles (25 of 236 points). Now the neckline sits at the neck-side point found from the body sections (1.480 m on the default body). The shoulders follow the body sections with 8 mm of ease. In the simulation mesh the shoulder rings are spaced evenly along the outline. The armhole top is placed where the garment's shoulder width meets the body piece, and drop-shoulder garments get the remaining width added to the sleeve. The static (non-draped) shape still covers each shoulder height and the row 2 cm below it, because the 2 cm section table underestimates the rounded shoulder top. All 138 automated tests pass. Trouser photo silhouette (2026-10-03, branch `3d-fit-upgrade`): `analyzeBottomSilhouette` in `lib/seller/photo-silhouette.ts` reads a front flat-lay of trousers. It finds the waist width (median of the top 1–4% of rows) and the crotch (the first of four rows in a row where a leg piece lies on each side of the centre). It then rejects the photo unless the legs stay separate on at least 85% of the rows below. It also measures the widest point above the crotch and each leg's width at 9 heights, as ratios to the thigh. Synthetic flat-lays (straight, tapered to 59% of the thigh, flared to 127%, shorts) were measured within 3–7% of the drawn proportions. Legs laid touching and a skirt shape were rejected with a reason. Through the seller form in the browser, a synthetic tapered flat-lay PNG (drawn crotch 30%, hem 64% of the thigh, hip 1.17 × waist, length 2.67 × waist) was measured as 31.7%, 65.8%, 1.14 and 2.60 after the form's downscaling to 420 px. Only the numbers were saved; they were kept through an edit, and the trousers appeared tapered in the 3D wardrobe and draped to the ankle. The test product was deleted afterwards. In 3D the leg widths are scaled by the thigh measurement, or 62% of the hip width when it is missing; missing length, rise and hip widths are filled from the ratios. A tapered photo-shaped trouser uncovered 1.3–3.0% on the four test bodies (limit 4%). No real flat-lay trouser photo was available. The only real sample was a worn shot, which this method is not meant for. When the legs nearly touch below the crotch, the crotch is found a few rows low, which lengthens the rise slightly. How closely the 3D trousers match a real photo has not been scored.

Drape speed (2026-10-03, branch `3d-fit-upgrade`): almost all of the time was the simulation loop (about 1.5–1.6 s per garment in Node; setup and the distance field took 0.1–0.2 s). Compared with a long, stiff reference run (1.6 s, 900 steps per second, four stretch passes), the result after 0.6 s of simulated time was as close as after 0.9 s: mean distance 19.6 mm against 19.2 mm for the tee and 19.1 mm against 19.2 mm for the slacks. The simulated time was therefore cut to 0.6 s. Fewer steps or fewer stretch passes made the cloth stretchier (3.0–3.4% against 2.3%) and were not used, and over-relaxation made it oscillate (12–22% stretch with two passes). Three code changes don't change the method. Each constraint's mass split is computed once. Particles far from the body and floor skip collision checks for as many steps as they cannot reach it (at most six). Bending is solved every other step at double strength. Alternating the old and new solver in one process, one tee drape went from 1.18–1.41 s to 0.97–1.10 s. When a top is worn over trousers, each mesh's distance field is now built only in a box around that mesh, so belt loops and the fly no longer sweep the whole grid. That cut the top's setup from about 1.5 s in the browser to 0.26 s. The worker now starts and loads the mannequin when the 3D view opens. While computing it sends 12 intermediate shapes, so the cloth is seen settling. In the browser on this Mac, tee and slacks together went from about 2.5 s + 3.1 s to 1.1 s + 1.15 s. All drape tests still pass: shoulders 1/236 and 6/246 uncovered on the default and 185 cm broad bodies, and 0/795 torso on both. Phone performance has not been measured.

## Accuracy limits

No paired real-person measurements, garment patterns, fabric parameters, or physical try-on study were supplied. Automated tests establish software behavior only; they do not establish real-world body-measurement or fit accuracy. Every estimate is labelled unvalidated in the UI.

Photo geometry tests use synthetic silhouettes and known landmarks. End-to-end MediaPipe detection and real-person photos require separate acceptance testing; no actual accuracy score is claimed.

## Privacy implementation

Photo files are held in component memory/object URLs and transferred as ImageBitmap objects to a local Web Worker. The Worker fetches only same-origin model/WASM assets, reads image pixels locally, and returns derived dimensions. Object URLs are revoked and the Worker is terminated on close. The only persistent value is the normalized BodyProfile. No analytics, image upload API or user-image server storage was added.
